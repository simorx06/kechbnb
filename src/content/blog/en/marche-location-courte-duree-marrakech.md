Marrakech welcomes several million international tourists each year, and the proportion of travellers choosing private accommodation via platforms like Airbnb continues to grow. In 2025, the short-term rental market in the ochre city offers real opportunities — provided you understand its specifics.

## A structurally growing market

Tourism in Marrakech benefits from solid international demand, driven by its global reputation, rich heritage and gastronomy. European travellers — French, British, Spanish, German — represent the majority of visitors, with a marked seasonality between October and April.

The growth of direct flight connections from dozens of European cities has made the destination more accessible. This accessibility directly benefits Airbnb landlords, who capture clients that would traditionally stay in hotels.

## Occupancy rates and revenue: what the data shows

For well-positioned and correctly managed properties, occupancy rates in Marrakech can exceed 80% during high season. A well-equipped apartment in the Gueliz or Hivernage district generates between €1,200 and €2,500 per month net, depending on size, quality and pricing management.

Medina riads — with terraces, patios or characteristic views — show even stronger performance, with nightly rates reaching €150 to €400 depending on guest capacity.

## The most promising neighbourhoods

**Medina**: riads and apartments in the historic lanes attract travellers seeking authenticity. Demand is strong, unit prices are high, but operational management (access, logistics) is more demanding.

**Gueliz and Hivernage**: these modern neighbourhoods appeal to business travellers and families. More standardised accommodation, but easier accessibility and consistent demand throughout the year.

**Palmeraie**: villas and properties with pools attract an upmarket group clientele. More pronounced seasonality, but very significant nightly revenues.

## Seasonality and managing the slow periods

High season broadly runs from October to May, with notable peaks during the Christmas and New Year holidays and in spring (March–April). July–August is low season: high temperatures reduce standard tourist footfall, although the MICE market (seminars, corporate events) can partially compensate.

The key to smoothing out the troughs? Aggressive pricing during low season, combined with platform visibility optimisation.

## What this means for property owners

A well-located, well-photographed, well-managed and correctly priced property can generate revenues 30 to 50% higher than a standard long-term rental. The differential is real — but it requires active, responsive management.
